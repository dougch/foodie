Ratatouille

Layered squash and potato bake that looks great.

Frenchside

This goes over well as a vegetable side for people who wouldn't normally eat squash. And it's pretty, so major points at a potluck or Turkey Day.

Ingredients

  • 4–5 small to medium Yukon Gold potatoes (or other waxy, thin-skinned potato)
  • 2 yellow crookneck squash
  • 2 medium zucchini
  • 2 C red sauce
  • 1/2 C grated Parmesan cheese
  • salt
  • olive oil
  • fresh basil, oregano or thyme (optional)

Equipment

  • Mandoline

Steps

  1. Cut the potatoes lengthwise, with the goal of making them roughly the same half-moon shape as the zucchini when sliced.
  2. Set your mandoline to about 3–4 mm, the goal being to have the potato slices be just a little stiff after parboiling.
  3. Put a pot of water on the stove for boiling the potatoes.
  4. Slice the potatoes on the mandoline and place them in the boiling water for about 5 minutes. We don't want them fully cooked; they just need more time than the squash. Drain and rinse the potatoes with cold water to stop the cooking process.
  5. While the potatoes cook, cut the zucchini and yellow squash lengthwise and slice them on the mandoline, trying to make uniform half-moon shapes.
  6. Arrange the squash slices on a large countertop and sprinkle lightly with salt. Ideally, they should sweat for about 30 minutes; then blot them dry with paper towels. Since this is the only salt in the dish, we're not going to rinse off or remove what's left of the salt.
  7. Put some pasta sauce in the bottom of a casserole, to about 1/2 inch. Sprinkle Parmesan over the sauce, and the herbs, if using.
  8. Arrange the zucchini around the edge of the casserole, with the flat part of each slice immersed in sauce.
  9. Working in small batches, arrange the slices in rows: zucchini, potato, yellow squash, potato, zucchini, etc. This is the tedious part and might require trimming and experimentation to make it look good.
  10. There will be leftover squash, especially the neck of the yellow squash and bits and ends that don't fit. I usually make a second, smaller dish with the odd bits finely chopped, but otherwise identical to the presentation dish.
  11. Drizzle a little olive oil on top and spread it out with a brush. Sprinkle a little more cheese and herbs on top.
  12. Cover with foil and bake at 350°F for about 30 minutes.

Because of how labor-intensive this is, I usually make it a day ahead and bake it, then reheat it before serving at 375°F for about 15 minutes, uncovered. It's done when the edges start to get crispy or a little brown.
