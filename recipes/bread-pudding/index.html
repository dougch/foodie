Bread Pudding

Rum-soaked fruit bread pudding for Thanksgiving.

Britishdessert

This was a Thanksgiving staple when the kids were little. The dried fruit adds texture and breaks up the heaviness.

A word on bread: the best for this recipe is something rich and cake-like, e.g. brioche, but white baguette is usually what I've got on hand. Obviously, wheat bread with nuts should be avoided, but anything else is game, including croissants.

Ingredients

  • 1 baguette, broken up into bite-sized pieces
  • 5 eggs
  • 1.5 C half and half
  • 1 C sugar
  • 1 t cinnamon
  • 1 t freshly grated nutmeg
  • 1 C dried cranberries (or raisins, cherries or blueberries)
  • orange zest
  • 1–2 C dark rum
  • pat of butter

Prep ahead

  • If the bread is fresh, you'll need to dry it out. The best method is to leave it out on the counter overnight. In a hurry, my go-to method is to set the oven to 200°F, spread the bread bits out on a cookie sheet, and place it in the oven for ~1 hour (turning off the oven ~30 minutes in to prevent browning).
  • Place the dried fruit in enough rum to cover. Add the orange zest and leave it overnight. The rapid rehydrating method is to cover and heat the fruit and rum in the microwave for 30 seconds and let it sit for as long as possible.

Steps

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F.
  2. Butter a 9x9 in or oval casserole.
  3. Strain the fruit from the rum, saving the liquid. Discard the orange zest.
  4. Mix the eggs, sugar, half and half, and 1 t of the rum/fruit juice.
  5. Place the dry bread in the baking dish and sprinkle with cinnamon and fresh nutmeg.
  6. Gently pour the egg mixture over the bread.
  7. Evenly sprinkle the fruit over the bread; it should (mostly) sink.
  8. Bake for 45 minutes to 1 hour, until a knife comes out clean and the bread on top has slightly browned. Give it ~30 minutes to cool.
